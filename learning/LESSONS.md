## FRA-01 — What a fraction means

Competency: **F01.fraction_meaning**.

Choose one whole and split it into equal-sized parts. The denominator counts how many such parts make one whole. The numerator counts how many you take.

For 1/3, split a rectangle into three equal pieces and shade one. Two rectangles of different whole sizes need not represent equal amounts even if both say 1/3.

Split each third into two equal parts. The same shaded amount is now 2/6, so 1/3 = 2/6. The amount stays the same; only its name changes.

A denominator cannot be zero: division by zero does not describe a valid equal partition. You do not need decimals, negative numbers or powers today.

**Worked example:** One of three equal pieces is 1/3. Split all three pieces in half: two of six equal pieces are shaded, so 1/3 = 2/6.

**Practise:** On paper, draw 2/3 of a rectangle. Label the top and bottom numbers. Draw 1/2 and 3/6 using the same-sized whole.

**Discover:** Repartition your own drawing. Give the same shaded amount two fraction names. Explain the equal-parts rule.

**Selected reference:** [§4.1 meaning and equivalent-fraction subsections only; skip readiness quiz](https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/4-1-visualize-fractions).

## FRA-02 — Adding and subtracting fractions

Competency: **F01.fraction_operations**.

For 1/4 + 2/4, the pieces have the same size. Count three quarters: 3/4.

For 1/2 + 1/4, first split the half into two quarters. Then 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4.

The denominator describes the size of the pieces; adding it would silently change the unit. Subtraction uses the same common-unit idea.

**Worked example:** 1/2 + 1/4 = 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4. A whole plus nothing is still a whole; 1/2 + 1/2 = 1, which refutes adding numerators and denominators together.

**Practise:** Draw and calculate 1/3 + 1/6. Then 3/4 − 1/4. Explain which pieces you counted.

**Discover:** Construct a counterexample to “add tops and add bottoms.” Say exactly why it fails.

**Selected reference:** [§4.4 common denominators, then §4.5 initial different-denominator examples](https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/4-5-add-and-subtract-fractions-with-different-denominators).

## FRA-03 — Multiplying and dividing fractions

Competency: **F01.fraction_operations**.

Half of three quarters is three eighths: (1/2) × (3/4) = 3/8. Draw a rectangle split in both directions.

How many quarter-units fit in a half? Two, so (1/2) ÷ (1/4) = 2.

Dividing by a nonzero fraction equals multiplying by its reciprocal. Numerator/denominator cancellation needs genuine common factors; never cancel across a sum.

**Worked example:** (2/3) × (3/4) = 6/12 = 1/2. Also (3/4) ÷ (1/2) = (3/4) × 2 = 3/2.

**Practise:** Calculate (2/3) × (3/5) and (1/2) ÷ (1/4). Draw the second as a counting question.

**Discover:** Can dividing a positive number make it larger? Give an example and explain the unit being counted.

**Selected reference:** [§4.2, one operation at a time](https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/4-2-multiply-and-divide-fractions).

## DEC-01 — Decimals name fractional parts

Competency: **F01.decimals**.

0.3 means three tenths: 3/10. 0.25 means twenty-five hundredths: 25/100 = 1/4.

Write 0.5 as 5/10 and 0.50 as 50/100. These represent the same amount.

Align equal place values when adding. Decimal multiplication and division must preserve the scale, not just the visible digits.

**Worked example:** 0.2 + 0.45 = 0.20 + 0.45 = 0.65. Half of 0.4 is 0.2; 0.8 ÷ 0.2 = 4.

**Practise:** Convert 3/4 to a decimal. Calculate 0.4 + 0.35, 0.6 × 0.5 and 0.9 ÷ 0.3 on paper.

**Discover:** Explain why writing an extra zero at the end of a decimal need not change its value.

**Selected reference:** [§§5.1–5.3 place value, operations and conversion subsections](https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/5-1-decimals).

## RAT-01 — Ratios, rates and percentages

Competency: **F01.ratios_percent**.

With 2 red tokens and 3 blue tokens, red:blue is 2:3. Red as a fraction of all tokens is 2/5, because there are five tokens in total.

A unit rate compares an amount per one unit, such as 12 kilometers in 3 hours giving 4 kilometers per hour.

Percent means per hundred. 25% = 25/100 = 1/4, so 25% of 80 equals 20. The reference amount matters.

**Worked example:** 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%. In 3 red and 2 blue tokens, red:blue = 3:2, while red/total = 3/5.

**Practise:** Find 10% of 60. With 4 red and 6 blue tokens, write red:blue and red/total. Keep the comparisons separate.

**Discover:** A 20% decrease then a 20% increase do not generally cancel. After learning percent changes, test this on 50 and explain the changing base.

**Selected reference:** [§5.6 ratios/unit rates and §6.1 percent; §6.2 percent changes later](https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/6-1-understand-percent).

## INT-01 — Negative numbers and signed operations

Competency: **F01.signed_numbers**.

Place zero on a line. Positive numbers lie to its right; negative numbers lie to its left. −2 is greater than −5 because it lies further right.

Starting at −2 and moving five units right gives −2 + 5 = 3. Subtracting three from two gives 2 − 3 = −1.

Multiplication sign rules must agree with distribution and additive inverses. For example, 0 = (−2)×(3 + (−3)) forces (−2)×(−3) = 6. Divide only by nonzero numbers.

**Worked example:** −3 + 5 = 2; 4 − 7 = −3; (−2)×(−3) = 6; (−8)÷2 = −4.

**Practise:** Draw −3 and −1, then calculate −4 + 6, 3 − 8 and (−3)×(−2). Explain each direction/sign.

**Discover:** Create two examples where a number with a larger absolute magnitude is actually smaller.

**Selected reference:** [§§3.1–3.4; one operation at a time](https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/3-1-introduction-to-integers).

## POW-01 — Powers and grouping

Competency: **F01.powers_order**.

3² means 3×3 = 9. It does not mean 3×2.

Do grouped operations first, then powers, then multiplication/division from left to right, then addition/subtraction from left to right.

2 + 3×4 = 14, while (2+3)×4 = 20. −(2²) = −4, while (−2)² = 4. Zero/negative exponents and roots remain separate later lessons.

**Worked example:** 2³ = 2×2×2 = 8. Parentheses make the base explicit: (−3)² = 9, while −(3²) = −9.

**Practise:** Calculate 3², 2+3×4, (2+3)×4, −(2²) and (−2)². Rewrite each power as multiplication.

**Discover:** Invent two expressions with the same digits but different grouping and different values.

**Selected reference:** [§2.1 exponent/order subsections only](https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/2-1-use-the-language-of-algebra).

## ALG-01 — Variables, expressions and equations

Competency: **F02.notation**.

In this lesson a variable is a symbol for a number. An expression, such as 3x+2, represents a value. An equation, such as 3x+2=14, states equality.

To evaluate an expression, replace each occurrence of a variable by the given number, keeping grouping clear. At x=4, 3x+2 = 3×4+2 = 14.

In Python, = assigns a value to a name; it does not solve an algebraic equation.

**Worked example:** At z=3, 5z+1 = 16. The expression 5z+1 and the equation 5z+1=16 play different roles.

**Practise:** Evaluate 2x+1 at x=0 and x=4. Write an expression describing three groups of a number plus two.

**Discover:** Compare (x+2)+3 with x+5. Explain why two examples alone do not prove an identity for every x.

**Selected reference:** [§1.2 expression/equation definitions](https://openstax.org/books/elementary-algebra-2e/pages/1-2-use-the-language-of-algebra).

## ALG-02 — Preserving equality

Competency: **F02.equality**.

For x+5=12, subtract 5 from both sides to obtain x=7. Check by substitution: 7+5=12.

Adding or subtracting the same number on both sides preserves the solution set. Multiplication/division by a nonzero number is reversible.

Multiplication by zero can lose information. Division by zero is undefined. Learn distribution and equations on both sides later through MAT-ALG.

**Worked example:** For t+3=11, subtract 3 from both sides: t=8. Substitution checks the result.

**Practise:** Solve x−4=9 and 3x=18. Explain why your operation preserves the same solutions.

**Discover:** Show how changing only one side can destroy equality using a numerical example.

**Selected reference:** [§2.1 first equality examples; §2.2 multiplication/division next](https://openstax.org/books/elementary-algebra-2e/pages/2-1-solve-equations-using-the-subtraction-and-addition-properties-of-equality).
