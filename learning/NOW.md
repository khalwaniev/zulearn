# Today: understand what a fraction means

**You need paper and a pencil.** About 25–35 minutes is a convenient starting block, not a deadline. Only addition, subtraction, multiplication and division are assumed. No setup, diagnostic examination or record form comes first.

## Learn

Choose one whole and divide it into equal-sized parts. The **denominator** counts how many such parts make one whole; the **numerator** counts how many you take.

Draw a rectangle, split it into three equal pieces, and shade one. You shaded **1/3**. Now split every piece in half. The same shaded amount is **2/6**. Therefore **1/3 = 2/6**: the amount stayed the same while its name changed.

The reference whole and equal-sized parts matter. One third of a small rectangle need not equal one third of a larger rectangle. A zero denominator is undefined; it is not a valid way to partition a whole.

If needed, paste the [short tutor instructions](CHATGPT_TUTOR.md) into ChatGPT Web and ask it to teach this explanation one step at a time. Ask for a worked drawing explanation when confused; you do not need to discover everything unaided during teaching.

## Practise on paper

1. Draw **2/3**, and say what each number counts.
2. Draw **1/2** and **3/6** using equal-sized wholes. Explain why the shaded amounts agree.
3. Make your own repartition: give one shaded amount two fraction names. Explain why unequal pieces cannot simply be counted as equal shares.
