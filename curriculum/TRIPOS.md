# Zulearn Scientific AI Tripos (ZSAT)

**Status: designed, not attempted or accredited.** Four levels. Adapted from Cambridge Tripos *assessment structure* and Math 55 *proof culture*; no assertion of equivalent difficulty or credentials.

## Eligibility and prerequisite diagnostics
A first: independent linear algebra, derivatives, probability, discrete mathematics and Python. Place by performance, not by prestige or time served. Unseen and retained exams are mandatory. Do not use AI on exams.

## Part I — rigorous foundations (target 12–20 weeks, diagnostic dependent)
- M1 Proof language, logic, quantifiers, induction, contradiction, inequalities, discrete mathematics.
- M2 Linear algebra: linear maps, spectral theorem, SVD, matrix norm, condition numbers, orthogonal projections, matrix calculus.
- M3 Multivariable calculus, gradients, Jacobians, Hessians, constrained optimization.
- M4 Probability: measure-aware intuition, conditioning, distributions, inequalities, Markov chains.
- M5 Algorithms: graph search, DPs, recurrences, complexity, data structures, matching.
- M6 Scientific computing: floating point, numerical linear algebra, automatic differentiation tests.
**Exam**: two three-hour closed-book papers, plus a 90-minute from-scratch implementation; oral proof defense.

## Part II — graduate-relevant ML mathematics (target 16–28 weeks)
- M7 Statistical inference, likelihood, M-estimation, confidence intervals, bootstrap and dependence.
- M8 Convex optimization, duality, KKT, first/second-order and stochastic methods.
- M9 Information theory, KL divergence, entropy, maximum entropy, variational inference.
- M10 Statistical learning, generalization, empirical process and concentration basics.
- M11 Kernel methods, Gaussian processes, matrix factorization, graphical models and EM.
- M12 Deep learning theory: backprop, conditioning, initialization, residual networks, attention, training dynamics.
**Exam**: two three-hour mixed derivation/proof papers, 24-hour research-code take-home, oral failure-analysis defense.

## Part III — scientific-mathematics specialization (target 20–36 weeks)
- M13 3D geometry, rigid motions, groups, rotations, SE(3) and equivariance.
- M14 ODEs, PDEs (selected), stochastic differential equations, simulation and inverse problems.
- M15 Bayesian inference, uncertainty, hierarchical models, experimental design, causal inference essentials.
- M16 Molecular mechanics basics: energies, gradients, thermodynamics, statistical mechanics, geometric constraints.
- M17 Inverse imaging: MRI reconstruction, tomography, variational optimization and regularization.
- M18 Sequence/graph mathematics: HMMs, dynamic programming for alignment, ontology DAGs, message passing.
**Exam**: two three-hour papers, 72-hour scientific modeling challenge with known and hidden test cases, oral review.

## Part IV — research qualifier (repeat until externally defensible)
Independently: (1) critically review 3 primary papers, (2) reproduce 1 modest result, (3) propose controlled extension, (4) present full methods and failure cases, (5) answer external critique.

## Passing standard
Design benchmark: >=75% on unseen written problems and no severe correctness gaps; reproducible code must pass checks. At least one second examiner should review major proof or research claims where feasible. This is a **local standard**, not validated equivalence to university exams.
