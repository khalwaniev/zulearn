## Definitive curriculum architecture

The authoritative design uses **one competency registry and one navigation spine**. Deep-Path is retained as the primary ML learning backbone because the supplied catalogue now spans classical ML, deep learning, LLMs, world models, RL, GNNs, inference, CUDA/Triton, compilers, safety, algorithms and related foundations rather than merely a small coding-problem bank. TensorTonic is assigned as a complementary source of alternative implementations, transfer problems, systems/GPU work and paper implementation where it adds demonstrably different evidence of competence. External courses and textbooks enter only when they fill a prerequisite or theory gap, or provide an independent mastery test. citeturn969264view1turn969264view2

The mathematical bridge is deliberately shorter than a conventional mathematics degree. ML need not wait for real analysis, abstract algebra or advanced differential equations. Entry into serious ML study requires demonstrated algebra/functions/logarithms, elementary trigonometric fluency, vectors and matrices, derivatives and the chain rule, elementary probability, and sufficient Python/NumPy competence to express the mathematics computationally. Linear algebra, multivariable calculus, probability/statistics, optimisation, discrete mathematics/proofs and numerical analysis then deepen in parallel with ML. Real analysis, sophisticated stochastic processes, differential geometry, abstract algebra, dynamical systems and algorithmic information theory become research-triggered specialisations unless a diagnostic or research problem proves them necessary. The external bridge uses selected portions of MIT 18.06/18.02/18.065/6.042J, Harvard Stat 110 and Stanford EE364A rather than forcing full duplicate course sequences. These choices are consistent with the repository's existing selective-course policy. citeturn969264view1turn554190view3

The dependency graph therefore proceeds roughly from foundational symbolic/numerical fluency → proof and programming → linear algebra/calculus/probability → classical ML and scientific inference → deep learning/autodiff/optimisation → sequence models/transformers/generative models → RL/planning/world models → algorithms/TCS and systems → scientific methodology → the five research specialisations. Research activity does **not** wait until the end: literature criticism, reproduction discipline, baseline construction, failure analysis and small research questions begin once the corresponding prerequisites are secure. That is consistent with Zulearn's existing research standard, which requires explicit hypotheses, provenance, leakage/shift checks, frozen validation, uncertainty, ablations, negative controls, failure analysis and reproducibility rather than merely running architectures. citeturn835539view4

The five research tracks are consequently specialised branches of a common core, not five separate curricula. AGI/world-model work emphasises representation, sequential prediction, planning, memory, continual learning and meta-learning; RSI adds formal self-reference, optimisation, program search, meta-learning, self-generated training signals, evaluation under distribution shift and safety constraints; reasoning adds algorithms, search, program synthesis, logic, complexity and formal verification; mechanistic interpretability adds transformer internals, linear algebra, causal intervention, representation geometry and rigorous evaluation; AI for Science adds numerical modelling, uncertainty, domain science, graphs/equivariance and scientific validation. Protein work additionally requires sequence biology, protein biochemistry, structural biology, thermodynamics/biophysics, evolutionary information, molecular representations, alignment/homology, structure/function ontologies and benchmark-specific leakage discipline rather than treating CASP or CAFA as ordinary supervised-learning contests. citeturn554190view4turn835539view4

## Diagnostic, mastery and learning protocol

No mathematical or computational mastery beyond the learner's reported arithmetic fluency is assumed. The diagnostic therefore uses **adaptive short packets** rather than an enormous preliminary examination. It starts with algebra/functions and programming foundations, escalates into proof, linear algebra, calculus, probability/statistics, algorithms and scientific reasoning, and only opens advanced packets when earlier prerequisites are demonstrated. Every packet distinguishes computational fluency, conceptual understanding, proof/reasoning and transfer. Programming portions are closed to AI-generated solutions, and advanced exemptions require evidence from unseen tasks rather than degree titles, publications or previous AI-assisted outputs.

The local diagnostic rubric is deliberately an operational decision rule rather than a claim of a psychometrically validated universal mastery percentage. This corrects a recurrent risk in curriculum design: arbitrary thresholds can be useful administratively, but they should not be misrepresented as scientifically optimal. Zulearn's existing mastery-gate philosophy already requires unseen closed-book work, implementation, assumptions/failure analysis and delayed retesting; the new diagnostic converts that philosophy into an executable branching assessment. citeturn554190view0

The learning protocol uses retrieval, spacing, interleaving and feedback conditionally rather than as slogans. Retrieval practice has positive transfer on average, but transfer varies with factors including successful initial retrieval and the relation between practice and transfer tasks. citeturn217008search0 Distributed practice has strong overall evidence, but the useful spacing interval depends jointly on the retention interval and the inter-study interval; there is no single scientifically optimal review schedule. citeturn217008search1 Interleaving also has heterogeneous effects that depend on task structure and similarity, so it is prescribed primarily after initial discrimination-relevant concepts have been learned rather than from the first exposure. citeturn217008search2 Productive-failure sequences can outperform instruction-first sequences under appropriate conditions, but the curriculum uses bounded generation attempts followed by explicit instruction rather than unguided discovery. citeturn217008search4

Feedback is treated as information for correcting models and strategies, not as a generic positive intervention: meta-analytic evidence shows meaningful heterogeneity and some negative effects depending on how feedback is delivered. citeturn719389search0 Deliberate practice is retained as an important mechanism but not elevated into a theory that explains all expert performance; the classic meta-analysis reports substantially different variance explained across domains and very little in some professional settings. citeturn719389search2 Sleep is protected as part of the curriculum rather than treated as expendable study time because experimental sleep-restriction evidence shows impairment of memory formation. citeturn719389search5

AI is permitted for explanations, adversarial critique, hints, test generation after solutions are hidden, debugging assistance and literature navigation, but not as evidence that the learner possesses a competency. Recent randomised educational experiments provide encouraging evidence that carefully structured AI tutoring can improve learning in particular settings, but they do not justify assuming broad independent competence from AI-assisted performance. citeturn719389search7 The curriculum therefore maintains closed-book derivation, from-scratch implementation, unseen transfer and delayed independent assessment as the final mastery evidence.

## Adversarial findings and overlooked competencies

Several competencies deserved stronger status than they received in earlier versions of Zulearn. The new registry explicitly elevates **measurement and data-generating-process reasoning**, numerical conditioning and floating-point error, calibration and uncertainty, causal inference, selection and adaptive-analysis bias, computational complexity, search/program synthesis, software testing and experimental provenance, ontology-aware evaluation for biological function, and homology/temporal leakage in computational biology. These are not ornamental additions: each can invalidate research conclusions even when model implementation is technically correct. Zulearn's own research protocol already makes many of the corresponding validity checks mandatory, which supports their promotion into the taught competency graph rather than leaving them as implicit research habits. citeturn835539view4

Conversely, several prestigious topics are **not** compulsory foundations. Full real-analysis sequences, broad abstract algebra, Olympiad training, exhaustive Putnam preparation, advanced geometry, large competitive-programming ladders and unrelated systems topics are excluded from the common core unless they become prerequisite bottlenecks for an actual research question. MathNet, AoPS, OTIS and Putnam are used selectively for proof discovery and non-routine transfer; their difficulty is not treated as evidence that they produce broad AI-research transfer. The repository already warns against claiming equivalence between its locally designed “Putnam-style/IMO-style” assessments and official competition standards. citeturn969264view1turn835539view1

The strongest unresolved curriculum uncertainty is not a missing theoretical subject but **item-level resource provenance**. Deep-ML now has sufficient supplied evidence for concept-level and substantial item-level allocation, whereas current TensorTonic raw identifiers are missing. Premium/access constraints also remain potentially relevant for some Deep-ML projects. The curriculum therefore freezes competency architecture now but leaves those resource substitutions conditional: if an inaccessible primary exercise is encountered, substitute a resource testing the same registry node rather than redesigning the curriculum.

## Freeze decision

The architecture is now sufficiently coherent to stop curriculum-search and begin execution. That does **not** mean it has been proven globally optimal; Zulearn's own completeness audit correctly distinguishes an evidence-informed curriculum from an experimentally optimised one. citeturn835539view1 The appropriate stopping rule is therefore to freeze the dependency graph, mastery model, platform roles and research-specialisation structure, then personalise only the path through that graph using diagnostic evidence.

Future changes should require one of four triggers: a demonstrated missing prerequisite, measured failure on independent transfer or delayed mastery tests, a concrete requirement arising from active research, or materially stronger/new scientific evidence. Platform novelty, newly released courses, badges, paper volume and competition hype are insufficient reasons by themselves. This preserves the central objective of the programme: not encyclopaedic prerequisite accumulation, but increasing capacity to derive, implement, test, criticise and ultimately invent the knowledge needed for unsolved scientific problems.
