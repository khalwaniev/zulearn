Five branches share the common core. Maintain **one active research question**; the other four are conditional queues. This file preserves the recovered thematic conclusions, adds explicit paper activities and original study contracts, and does not claim an exhaustive or current laboratory bibliography. A historical paper is selected for a capability, not because it is the latest result.

## Research fit and common prerequisites

The current official KAUST profile and Schmidhuber's author-maintained research pages support thematic connections to sequence learning, meta-learning, world models, search and self-improvement. This is a research-fit inference. No private interview syllabus, admission guarantee, funding promise or future supervision availability has been inferred. Recheck official admissions and research opportunities when planning an actual application.

All branches need scientific question formulation, data-generating-process reasoning, trustworthy splits, statistics, independent programming, computational feasibility, reproducibility, criticism and communication. Read a paper as early as its prerequisites permit; do not wait for every advanced mathematical branch. A paper may be skimmed for its question before its full derivation becomes accessible, but label that stage honestly.

## Five research branches — entry, depth and a bounded first study

The registry's **entry** column governs first lessons. The deeper prerequisites below apply to the specified research claim, not to every introductory activity. Keep one active branch/question and at most one or two anchor papers active; the remaining references are available when a gap appears.

| Branch | Accessible entry | Depth required for the selected claim | Bounded first artifact and discriminating controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| AGI/world models | Tabular prediction/planning after finite MDPs; a small memory/generalization example | Partial observability, representation learning, neural dynamics, search and held-out task acquisition as the hypothesis needs | One finite task family comparing true-model planner, learned-model planner and a simple baseline under equal interactions/compute; separate prediction loss, rollout error and real task utility |
| RSI/meta-learning | A simple adaptation or iterative program-reuse experiment; distinguish training from evaluation | Task distributions, adaptation gradients, evaluator validity, code/search reuse; computability/utility/provability for formal guarantees | One tiny adaptation/reuse hypothesis, frozen held-out families and matched total effort; compare with repeated tuning without the proposed self-change and disclose manual interventions |
| Mathematical/algorithmic reasoning | Tiny typed DSL, search/CSP, ambiguity and a checked finite verifier | Formal languages, complexity, synthesis, learned proposals, verifier-guided search and evidence against memorization | One bounded synthesis task with known oracle; compare enumeration/prior/learned component at equal budgets; include multiple consistent programs, discriminating queries and out-of-family tests |
| Mechanistic interpretability | Transformer/attention components followed by one probe or toy intervention | Residual-stream/feature geometry, causal identification, distributed representations and intervention artifacts for mechanism claims | One narrow behavior in a tiny model: matched clean/corrupt inputs, intervention/resampling/negative controls and rescue/alternative explanations; correlation, causal contribution and a unique explanation are different claims |
| AI for Science | Domain vocabulary, measurement and entity/time-leakage reasoning before complex structure modeling | Chosen biology/chemistry/physics, graph/geometry, uncertainty and experimental-validation requirements | One domain-appropriate simple baseline with temporal/entity/homology controls, uncertainty and physical/domain checks; do not train full AlphaFold or enter CASP/CAFA as a prerequisite to first scientific reasoning |

One valid negative result or identified ambiguity may be a useful outcome. A reproduction/extension needs the claim contract and externally defensible evidence; a small toy is labelled accurately. Foundational RL remains core, while large-scale self-play, specialist continuous control and every post-training algorithm are conditional. A learned world model need not be a video generator; representation learning can be self-supervised without defining a normalized generative density.

## Activity vocabulary

`R` read exact question/claims/methods/limitations; `D` derive central mathematics independently; `I` implement the relevant mechanism; `P` empirically reproduce a specified published claim under a documented protocol; `X` test a new, controlled extension. Each is a separate status. No P/X is mandatory for every paper. A small-data architecture build normally earns I, not P. A deliberately scaled study may be a **scaled replication of a narrowed claim** with explicit deviations; it must not be represented as a reproduction of the original headline result.

## Common original research sheets and contracts

**RES-QUESTION — question and criticism.** Make a one-page ledger of one paper's exact question, claim, evidence, assumptions, strongest competing explanation and smallest meaningful next experiment. Include one plausible negative outcome and what it would teach. Identify which part you cannot yet derive.

**RES-AGENT — agent reliability.** On a fixed set of locally defined code/data tasks, compare a simple no-agent baseline with a supervised tool-using system. Record tool calls, invalid outputs, runtime, manual interventions, permission boundaries and task success with a checked oracle. Hold out task families; test interruption/malformed-tool failures. Do not infer an agent's correctness from another agent agreeing with it.

**RES-ARTIFACT — artifact provenance.** Create a compact manifest of data revision/license, split entities, code revision, environment, config/seed, generated outputs and SHA-256 values. Re-execute one small result from that manifest. Record every deviation and manual step; do not include restricted data or credentials.

**RES-COMM — communication and defense.** Write 300–500 words answering question/method/evidence/limitations for your own artifact. Give a five-minute oral explanation of the central equation and answer two adversarial questions. The word/time counts are local practice bounds, not validated optimal doses. Keep claims at the narrowest supported level.

**RES-EFF — efficiency.** Prespecify a correct reference, data/model size and hardware. Measure end-to-end wall time, memory and a meaningful task-quality metric, including warm-up/synchronization as relevant. Separate kernel microbenchmark gains from end-to-end study gains; include data/preprocessing/tuning costs. Never count unattended training runtime as focused learning time.

**Reproduction contract.** Before P: select the exact published claim, source version, data/protocol, primary metric, baseline/tuning budget, expected compute, comparison/uncertainty and material deviations. Freeze analysis choices before the confirmatory comparison. Report every outcome and failure; full headline reproduction is optional when infeasible, but the label must change accordingly.

**Extension contract.** State a mechanism-based hypothesis and alternatives, one discriminating intervention, a minimum meaningful effect, controls, stop/budget rule and independent test data. Literature criticism and negative results can be useful; novelty and acceptance are not assumed. Repeated leaderboard tuning is not a substitute for controlled evidence.

## Distinct objectives: generation, SSL, post-training and verification

L16 generative modeling and L26 self-supervised representation learning have separate objectives and mastery evidence. A generative model concerns a sampling/density or latent generative objective. Self-supervision concerns targets constructed from the data; it may be generative, contrastive or predictive. Next-token prediction can be both generative and self-supervised, but solving one generative toy task does not establish contrastive/predictive representation mastery. Neither automatically establishes downstream scientific usefulness.

L27 covers the common post-training distinctions: supervised fine-tuning, reward-model learning from preferences, direct preference optimization and evaluation. Policy-gradient/PPO/GRPO implementation is a later branch needing its actual RL prerequisites; it is not a prerequisite to understanding a pairwise logistic/DPO loss. L28 covers verifier-guided reasoning/search and differentiates a checked outcome verifier, process feedback and a fallible learned judge. These nodes strengthen the target domains without requiring all named papers or large-model training.

## Methods worksheets — exact definitions

**RL-R1 — finite MDPs, values, tabular TD and planning.** This resolves the baseline's undefined R1 locator. (1) Specify a finite discounted MDP: state/action sets, transition probabilities, rewards, initial/terminal conditions, objective and 0≤γ<1. (2) In a one-state/one-action process with reward 2 each step and γ=1/2, derive v=2+v/2 and solve it; iterate T(v)=2+v/2 from v₀=0 and relate error to contraction. (3) In a separate observed transition with current estimate v(s)=1, next estimate v(s')=3, reward 2, γ=1/2 and step size α=0.2, hand-compute one TD(0) update using the old values. For a terminal next state use the declared zero continuation value. (4) Compare exact finite-state Bellman solutions, sampled Monte Carlo returns and tabular TD under a fixed data budget; quantify variability from actual executions. (5) Add a learned table model and a fixed number of planning updates; deliberately bias the model and test real-environment performance versus model-predicted utility. No RNN, Transformer or full convex-optimization course is needed. Neural function approximation/off-policy instability comes later in L19. Primary conceptual support: Sutton–Barto 2nd ed., selected finite-MDP/DP/TD/planning chapters, plus D069–075.

**ML-GEN — generative objective.** On a finite distribution, specify valid probabilities, compute a normalized likelihood and sample from it. Then reconstruct one latent/generative objective (MAT-VI supplies a tiny ELBO) or one diffusion objective only when needed. Separate density/sample quality from learned representation utility and downstream task evaluation. One selected method suffices; do not complete GAN, VAE, every diffusion family and video generation as a cumulative common-core gate.

**ML-SSL — independent representation evidence.** Define a prediction/contrastive target or two data views with explicit augmentations and label/data provenance. Compare the representation with a random/simple baseline under a matched training/evaluation budget. Include a collapsed/constant representation as a diagnostic and a held-out downstream or transfer test using only training-derived preprocessing. State which augmentations are scientifically valid and what information they remove. Do not treat “trained without labels,” a low pretext loss, generative likelihood or a guided implementation as full SSL mastery. Pick one objective from D039–040; no new mandatory paper reproduction is added.

**ML-POST — SFT, rewards and preferences.** (1) Define a tiny instruction/response task and train/evaluate splits; identify which tokens an SFT loss should include. (2) For a preferred/dispreferred response pair, model preference as σ(r_w−r_l), derive its negative log likelihood and explain why a learned reward may exploit length/style or label artifacts. (3) For β>0 and a fixed reference policy, derive the DPO loss −log σ(β[(log π(y_w|x)−log π_ref(y_w|x))−(log π(y_l|x)−log π_ref(y_l|x))]); state support, sequence/masking and preference-model assumptions rather than treating it as a universal truth. (4) Compare outcome correctness, held-out preference accuracy and distribution shift; no public-test tuning. (5) Only if a research question needs RL post-training, extend to policy-gradient/actor-critic/PPO/GRPO after L18–L20 and report the extra sampling/compute. D054–056 and the inspected TensorTonic SFT/preference examples provide practice. The relevant sections of [InstructGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155) or [DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290) are targeted method references, not two additional compulsory reproductions.

**ML-VERIFY — verifier-guided reasoning.** Use a finite arithmetic/DSL task family with a known checked oracle. Distinguish final-answer correctness from validity of intermediate steps, process supervision from outcome supervision, and a true finite verifier from a learned reward/judge. Compare a baseline generator/search, a verifier/reranker and one controlled variant with equal candidate/token/compute budgets and untouched task families. Introduce a faulty/permissive verifier to detect reward hacking or evaluator exploitation. Report search coverage, valid solutions, false acceptance, costs and ambiguous/no-solution/timeout cases separately. A learned judge's agreement is not a proof. [Let's Verify Step by Step](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050) is an optional targeted comparison of supervision choices; do not import its headline results into the toy study.

## Frontier depth without reading inflation

For each active study, use one anchor mechanism and a small set of discriminating controls before adding references. AGI asks about acquisition/generalization under fixed priors/budgets; RSI about a specified improvement mechanism and evaluator; reasoning about search/language/verifier roles; MechInt about intervention-supported explanations; scientific AI about measurement/domain validity and uncertainty. Further papers enter only to answer an actual unresolved question. The baseline 17-paper reference table is retained, not converted into 17 mandatory reproductions. The three post-training/verifier links and VI review are on-demand support, not an expanded reading quota.
